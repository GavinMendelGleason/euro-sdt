# Commission candidate pools: College 2024, DG/DDG management positions, EPSO

Probe date: 2026-11-03. Batch: `commission_pool`.

## Bottom line

- **College 2024-2029:** 27 seats, 27 formal nominees at the point the Council adopted the list (26 commissioners-designate + the re-elected President). The pool is one nominee per member state by Treaty design, not a competitive pool. One formally documented substitution (Slovenia) plus two reported or announced substitutions (France, Romania) mean up to 30 distinct individuals were put forward during formation.
- **Commission DG/DDG and other senior management posts:** 20 vacancy notices were published in 2024 (COM/2024/10443-10462): 2 Directors-General, 1 Deputy Director-General, 7 Directors, 5 Principal Advisers, 2 Heads of Representation and 3 other senior adviser/specialist posts. A further 11 notices covered EU agencies and other bodies (16 openings).
- **Applicant counts for Commission management posts are not public.** Neither the vacancy notices nor DG HR's 2024 Annual Activity Report publish applications, eligible candidates or shortlists. Only the selected appointee is announced. Status: `not_public`.
- **EPSO 2024:** 710 places requested and 774 laureates delivered in open competitions completed in 2024 (109%); 842 laureates from old-model competitions finalised; 8 new open competitions published; CAST Permanent held 190,014 applications from 111,125 candidates; 38,851 computer-based tests. EPSO does not run DG/DDG selections (`not_applicable`).

## Definitions used

- **Formal nominee:** a person whose name was formally submitted by a member state (or the European Council/Parliament for the President) for a seat. Announcements by a prime minister that are not confirmed as transmitted do not count as formal.
- **Announced candidate:** publicly named as intended nominee but no official transmission located.
- **Opening:** a seat or post to be filled, from the official vacancy list, not the number of applications.
- **Applicant/candidate:** a person who applied or was tested. Never inferred from openings.

## 1. College of Commissioners 2024-2029

Sources:
- Council of the EU, Decision ST-12442-2024 of 19 September 2024 adopting the list of persons proposed for appointment as members of the Commission: <https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-12442-2024-INIT/en/pdf>
- Council of the EU, press release 19 September 2024: <https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2024/09/19/council-adopts-the-list-of-candidates-proposed-for-appointment-as-members-of-the-commission>
- European Commission, archived "Commissioners-designate (2024-2029)": <https://commission.europa.eu/about/commission-2024-2029/commissioners-designate-2024-2029_en>

Figures:
- Council decision, Article 1: **25 named persons plus Kaja Kallas** as High Representative (appointed by the European Council on 24 July 2024) = **26**.
- Council decision, recital (2): the Commission consists of **one national of each Member State, including its President and the High Representative** = **27 seats**.
- President: Ursula von der Leyen, proposed by the European Council on 27 June 2024 and elected by the European Parliament on 18 July 2024 (recital (3)) = the 27th member.
- Commission page: **26** commissioners-designate (counted from 26 portfolio entries). It excludes the President.

### Substitutions during formation

| Member state | First formal nominee | Final nominee | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Slovenia | Tomaž Vesel (government decision 26 July 2024) | Marta Kos (government decision 9 September 2024) | Official gov.si releases for both |
| France | Thierry Breton (incumbent; announced as nominee July 2024) | Stéphane Séjourné (Council list, 19 September 2024) | Final name official; first nomination not located in an official French document |
| Romania | Victor Negrescu (announced by the Prime Minister, August 2024) | Roxana Mînzatu (Council list, 19 September 2024) | Final name official; no official transmission of Negrescu located |

Slovenia is fully documented by two official government decisions. France and Romania are `partial`: the final nominee is official, the earlier name rests on press reporting or a political announcement, not on a located official nomination document. Accordingly:

- **29 distinct formal nominees** if only Slovenia and France are counted as substitutions.
- **30** if the Romanian announcement was also formally transmitted.

The correct statement for analysis is therefore: "27 seats; 27 formal nominees at Council adoption; 29-30 distinct individuals formally or publicly put forward once substitutions are counted."

No applicant or shortlist data exists for commissioners: nomination is a member-state prerogative.

### Applicant counts

`not_public`. Evidence of absence:
- The DG HOME Director-General vacancy notice (COM/2024/10449) describes eligibility, pre-selection panel, CCA interview and shortlist stages but publishes no counts: `sources/official/probes/vacancy_dg_home_en.txt`.
- DG HR Annual Activity Report 2024 contains no application, candidate or shortlist counts for senior management; its only application statistic is the Junior Professionals Programme (1,750 applications for 50 places). Search of the full text for "senior management", "management post", "appoint" and "applicat" returns no such table.
- The "Documents for senior management selection procedures" page publishes only a privacy statement, an external-applicant guide and a 2017 policy compilation.

No formal candidate list is published either: the Commission announces only the person appointed. A Freedom of Information request would be required to obtain per-post application numbers.

### Appointment releases

`not_public` as a consolidated count. Appointments are announced ad hoc in Daily News items and individual press releases (e.g. "The European Commission appoints a new Director for its Directorate-General for Mobility and Transport", Daily News 29/05/2024, MEX/24/2986). No annual list or count of 2024 senior-management appointment releases was located. The press-corner search API ignored the date and document-type parameters tested, so a complete enumeration was not possible from that interface.

## 3. EPSO statistics 2024

Sources:
- EPSO Annual Activity Report 2024: <https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/c6b5889c-b855-49bc-9932-4c9ce2ac1d2b_en?filename=EPSO_AAR_2024%20bis.pdf>
- EPSO Annual Activity Report 2024 annexes: <https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/55407a84-4d32-4276-9ad7-2a555dc8d6b0_en?filename=EPSO_AAR_2024_annexes%20bis.pdf>
- DG HR Annual Activity Report 2024: <https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/add726fa-a314-4123-bc40-be9f4e00e27b_en?filename=HR_AAR_2024_final.pdf>

Exact figures:
- "In total, 774 laureates were delivered versus 710 laureates requested in EPSO open competitions completed during 2024" (AAR p.11). Delivery rate 109%.
- "Several large, multi-field 'old model' competitions were finalised providing a total of 842 laureates" (AAR p.4). This is a separate statement from the 774/710 comparison; do not add the two.
- "8 new open competitions published" in 2024 (AAR p.11).
- 1 internal competition for the Committee of the Regions with **72 successful candidates** (AAR p.11).
- CAST Permanent at end-2024: **190,014 applications corresponding to 111,125 candidates** (AAR p.11).
- **38,851 computer-based tests** administered for open competitions, CAST P and JPP (AAR p.11).
- Recruiters shortlisted **8,755 applications** and **8,208 tests** were delivered (AAR annexes, p.5).
- Junior Professionals Programme: **1,750 applications for 50 places**, two exercises in 2024; November 2024 intake had 968 applicants (DG HR AAR 2024, p.16). EPSO tested 1,223 JPP candidates (AAR p.11).

Caveats:
- The annexes' "42,606" applicants figure is the **2019 baseline**, not 2024. It must not be used as a 2024 count.
- EPSO publishes no per-competition applicant/place table in the 2024 AAR or annexes. Per-competition pages on EU Careers show the reference, grade, deadline and reserve-list validity, but not applicant numbers.
- EPSO does not run DG/DDG or other Commission management selections; those are internal Commission procedures (`not_applicable`).

## What failed or was blocked

- European Parliament discharge report A-10-2026-0081 and adopted text TA-10-2026-0126 returned empty responses (bot protection); no senior-management application data could be extracted there.
- EUR-Lex PDF endpoints returned empty bodies; the HTML reader worked for COM(2025) 423 (pensions), which is unrelated.
- Press-corner API: `text=` search is capped to a rolling recent window; `keyword=` ignores date/type parameters (`afterDateUTC`, `announcementType`, `docutype` had no effect), so 2024 appointment releases could not be enumerated.
- Romanian government (gov.ro) and Élysée search pages did not yield the 2024 nomination releases for Negrescu/Mînzatu or Breton/Séjourné; those substitutions remain `partial`.
- No official aggregate of applications to Commission senior management posts was found in DG HR AAR 2024, DG HR Management Plan 2024, the ECA Special Report 24/2024, or the Commission's "Key Figures" staff publication.
