# National CJEU nomination records — BG, RO, HU, SI, HR, EL, CY, MT (2023–2025)

Gap: `cjeu_candidate_pool`. Companion file: `national_cjeu_c.csv`.

## Method and definitions

Searched national nomination machinery (ministries of justice, judicial councils, official gazettes,
parliaments), plus the EU-level act that records the result (Council/OJ appointment decisions, CURIA press
releases). Four quantities are kept strictly apart, because they are not interchangeable:

- **opening / seats** — the post a state had to fill (expiry, death, resignation, or a new term).
- **applicants** — people who filed an application in a national competition.
- **candidates** — people a state formally put forward (nominee/shortlist).
- **judges** — people actually appointed by the Conference of Representatives.

The authoritative anchor for results is the OJ decision or CURIA press release; national documents establish
whether an application stage existed at all.

Only **Croatia** and **Slovenia** ran a public, advertised competition for a CJEU seat in 2023–2025. Neither
published applicant numbers. Bulgaria, Cyprus and Malta filled their seats by government nomination. Romania,
Hungary and Greece had no Court of Justice seat to fill in the window.

## Bulgaria

- **Opening.** Decision (EU) 2025/1551 records that Arabadjiev's mandate expired on 6 October 2024 (part of the
  13-seat 2024 renewal) and that the replacement term runs 15 September 2025 – 6 October 2030. The seat was
  therefore vacant for roughly eleven months.
- **Nomination.** Council of Ministers, 16 April 2025: approved Kornezov as Bulgaria's candidate on the joint
  proposal of the foreign and justice ministers (BTA, 16.04.2025). No call for applications, no shortlist and no
  applicant list appear in the record of that decision.
- **Appointment.** Decision (EU) 2025/1551 of 23 July 2025 (OJ L, 25.7.2025), after a favourable Article 255
  opinion. Kornezov moved from the General Court (judge since 19 September 2016) to the Court of Justice.
- **Assessment.** `not_public` for the pool: a single government nominee, no competition. Post-window reporting
  (Faktor, 25.06.2026) states the appointment was made "without a competition and without an announced
  procedure", and contrasts it with earlier Bulgarian CJEU selections run under a Council of Ministers decree
  published in the State Gazette — treat that characterisation as retrospective, not contemporaneous.
- **Useful benchmark (outside the window).** Bulgaria's 2026 competition for **two** General Court seats
  (Order No LS-13-52) drew **17 applications**, of which **13** were admitted to interview and **2** were ranked
  (Ivan Stoynev, Deyan Rusanov); six unsuccessful applicants appealed. This is the only hard applicant count
  found anywhere in the eight states, and it dates from 2026.

## Cyprus

- Lycourgos reappointed by decision of 8 November 2023 for 7 October 2024 – 6 October 2030 (Consilium press
  release, 08.11.2023; CURIA PR 174/24). No Cypriot public call, shortlist or applicant list was located; the
  renewal is a government proposal screened only by the Article 255 committee.

## Croatia

- **Call.** Public competition (*javni natječaj*) for **two** Croatian candidates for the General Court,
  Narodne novine 97/2024 of 16 August 2024, advert no. 5121; issued by the *Povjerenstvo za izbor kandidata
  Republike Hrvatske za suce i nezavisnog odvjetnika pri Sudu Europske unije* under the Government decision
  published in NN 117/12. Requirements: unquestionable independence; bar examination plus at least 12 years'
  legal experience; Croatian nationality; English and French. Deadline 30 days from publication (mid-September
  2024). Renewal applicants submit a list of cases; first-term applicants submit publications, a note on up to
  three sensitive cases and a motivation letter.
- **Outcome.** Government conclusion proposing **Tamara Perišin** (renewal) and **Tanja Pavelin** (first term)
  for 1 September 2025 – 31 August 2031, on a proposal from the Ministry of Justice. The document text is dated
  21 October 2024; press reported the closed-session decision on 31 October 2024, so the exact adoption date is
  ambiguous between the two. The conclusion records the competition and the opinion of the Sabor's European
  Affairs Committee as its basis. Appointed 11 June 2025; Pavelin replaced Vesna Tomljenović.
- **Gap.** The competition rules are public but the applicant pool is not: no list of applicants, no count, no
  ranking published. `not_public`/`partial` accordingly.

## Slovenia

- **Court of Justice.** Ilešič died on 20/21 June 2024. Delo (05.07.2024) reported that the Ministry of Justice
  would publish the call on Friday 12 July 2024 with a one-month deadline, and described the chain: Sodni svet
  opinion → Government opinion → President's proposal → National Assembly confirmation → Article 255 panel.
  **Marko Bošnjak** was appointed by decision of 11 June 2025, in office 12 June 2025 – 6 October 2027 (CURIA PR
  70/25). The call text itself and the Sodni svet opinion were **not located**; press lists of possible
  candidates (Vidmar, Accetto, Prek, Erbežnik, Trstenjak) were explicitly speculative.
- **General Court.** Call for **two** seats, Uradni list RS 96/2024 of 15 November 2024, file 110-2/2024-2030,
  under the ZPKSMS; deadline 13 December 2024; applications by e-mail to gp.mp@gov.si; French an advantage;
  balanced gender representation invoked. Outcome: **Maja Brkan** and **Damjan Kukovec** renewed for 1 September
  2025 – 31 August 2031 (CURIA PR 121/25). No applicant count published.

## Malta

- **Court of Justice.** Xuereb's term ended 6 October 2024. Malta nominated **Ramona Frendo** (General Court judge
  since 20 March 2019); approved by the Article 255 panel in May 2024 (reported 23.05.2024); appointed by decision
  of 22 May 2024, in office from 7 October 2024 (CURIA PR 174/24).
- **General Court.** Frendo's move vacated a General Court seat. The Prime Minister nominated former justice
  minister **Edward Zammit Lewis** in June 2024 (MaltaToday, 19.06.2024), over PN objection. The Article 255 panel
  gave a **negative** opinion in December 2024 and the nomination fell; the seat remained unfilled through 2025,
  and **Andria Buhagiar** was appointed in 2026. This is the only in-window case in the eight states where the
  EU-level screening visibly removed a national nominee.
- **Assessment.** Nominations are political, with a Maltese committee that "evaluates applications" but no
  published call, shortlist or applicant count located.

## Romania, Hungary, Greece — no vacancy in the window

- **Romania:** Spineanu-Matei, Judge at the Court of Justice since 7 October 2021. A government-approved
  selection procedure for a Court of Justice candidate was reported on 27 August **2026** (announcement 21 Sept,
  applications to 7 Oct, hearings 2–4 Nov, result 6 Nov, government proposal to the Council by 27 Nov). That is
  outside the window and is not recorded as in-window data.
- **Hungary:** Csehi, Judge at the Court of Justice since 7 October 2021. Hungarian appointments to the Court were
  reported in April **2026**, also outside the window.
- **Greece:** Gratsias, Judge at the Court of Justice since 7 October 2021; Advocate General Rantos since
  10 September 2020. No vacancy.

For all three, `not_applicable` is recorded for the *Court of Justice* seat only. No General Court appointment
for these states appears in the 2024–2025 decisions examined (22 May 2024, 2 July 2024, 2 Oct 2024, 26 Mar 2025,
11 June 2025), but 2023 General Court decisions were not enumerated, so a 2023 General Court vacancy for one of
them cannot be excluded on the present evidence.

## What failed / remains open

- **Slovenian Court of Justice call (July 2024):** not found. Uradni list 55/2024 (the 12 July 2024 issue) was
  checked and contains only domestic judicial and prosecutorial posts, so the call was evidently not gazetted
  there; the Ministry of Justice "javne objave" archive and the Sodni svet site were searched without retrieving
  the text; the Dz-rs.si evidence base returned only a session index.
- **Applicant counts:** none of the eight states published the number of applications for any 2023–2025 CJEU
  seat. Only Bulgaria's 2026 General Court competition gives a count (17).
- **Malta Government Gazette (30 April 2024):** the issue appears to carry a judicial-post notice, but gov.mt
  returns HTTP 403 to scripted and rendered fetches; content could not be verified and is therefore not used.
- **Romanian and Hungarian 2026 procedures** were located but deliberately excluded as out of window.
