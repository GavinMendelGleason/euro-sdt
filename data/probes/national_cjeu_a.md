# National CJEU nomination records, 2023-2025 — batch A

Ten member states: Austria, Belgium, France, Germany, Italy, Spain, Portugal, Netherlands, Luxembourg, Ireland. Question for each: when a Court of Justice or General Court seat was filled in 2023-2025, did the state publish a call for applications, an applicant count, a shortlist, or only the final nominee?

Data are in `data/probes/national_cjeu_a.csv`; source documents in `sources/official/probes/national_cjeu_a/`.

## The appointments that fall in the window

From CURIA press releases 174/24 and 121/25 and Council decision 2025/1185:

- **Court of Justice, 2024 renewal.** Renewed: Lycourgos, Passer, von Danwitz (DE), Ziemele, Jarukaitis, Kumin (AT). - **General Court, 2025 renewal.** Nineteen renewals, including Kingston and Mac Eochaidh (IE), Martín y Pérez de Nanclares and Sampol Pucurull (ES), Cassagnabère and Truchot (FR), Laitenberger and Steinfatt (DE). New: Bestagno and Pezzuto (IT).

## State by state

### Portugal — the only published candidate pool

The 2024 attempt is the one case in this batch where a shortlist is public.

- A public call was run; the evaluation panel put three names to the government: Gonçalo de Almeida Ribeiro, Carla Farinhas and Sofia Oliveira Pais. The Assembleia da República's European Affairs Committee heard them on 3 July 2024 and approved its report on 4 July (`sources/official/probes/national_cjeu_a/pt-ata-cae-12-2024.pdf`, minutes ATA 12/XVI, 1st SL). The three names are reported by Público/Lusa, 30 Oct 2024.
- The government formalised Almeida Ribeiro, then vice-president of the Constitutional Court. The Article 255 panel gave a negative opinion in September 2024: he did not meet the panel's 20-year professional-experience benchmark. The Foreign Minister called the rejection illegal in Parliament on 30 Oct 2024; a new candidate was announced in December 2024.
- A fresh call for the same post opened on 25 Aug 2026 (deadline 21 Sep 2026), again providing for a three-candidate shortlist, a hearing of all three by the AR European Affairs Committee, and then a government choice. Its selection panel includes Nuno Piçarra as the sitting Portuguese judge. [INFERENCE] The 2024 rejection did not remove him from office, and the 2026 call is the process that will produce his successor; the accessible Portuguese records name the post only as "juiz do Tribunal de Justiça da União Europeia" and do not state the term of office or the outgoing member, so the CSV records the court but not the seat.
- The 2026 call document is the clearest statement of the Portuguese model: public call, panel interview, three-name shortlist, parliamentary hearing, government selection, then an Article 255 request that the government justify its choice (`pt-convite-tjue-2026.pdf`).

### Netherlands — public call, confidential shortlist

- Vacancy posted 14 July 2023 via werkenvoornederland.nl, deadline 4 Sept 2023, interviews 11-12 Sept 2023. The post was for the term 7 Oct 2024 to 6 Oct 2030.
- The independent committee is the President of the Hoge Raad, the Vice-President of the Raad van State and a third member appointed by the ministers. The procedure agreement of 4 July 2023 (published to the States General) requires a recommendation of at least three people and states that the recommendation "is niet openbaar" — not public.
- Herke Kranenborg was first proposed; he withdrew, and the Council of Ministers approved Ben Smulders on 12 April 2024. No applicant count was published at any stage.

### Luxembourg — public calls, nominee only

- AG seat, 2023: call launched 28 June 2023; selection committee by ministerial decree of 18 Sept 2023; interviews in October; Dean Spielmann announced 25 Oct 2023.
- General Court seat, 2024: call of 26-27 April 2024 in six newspapers (Luxemburger Wort, Tageblatt, Quotidien, Lëtzebuerger Land, Zeitung vum Lëtzebuerger Vollek, WOXX), on the ministry website and in the Journal officiel; committee of 28 May 2024; interviews 8 July 2024; Raphaël Meyer announced 17 July 2024.
- In both cases the communiqué gives the sequence but no applicant number, no shortlist and no ranking.

### Austria — public expression of interest

The Ministerrat proposal of 6 Aug 2024 for Kumin's renewal records a "öffentliche Interessentensuche im elektronischen Verlautbarungs- und Informationssystem des Bundes" before the government decision. The document then names only Kumin; it gives no number of expressions of interest and no ranking. Consent of the Nationalrat Main Committee was sought under Art. 23c(2) B-VG. This is consistent with the Bundestag's description of Austria as a state that advertises, in contrast to Germany.

### France — call for the General Court seat only

- The General Court post vacated by Gervasoni's move to the Court of Justice was advertised by the Ministry for Europe and Foreign Affairs: "Appel à candidatures pour le poste de juge au Tribunal de l'Union européenne (15.06.24)", deadline 15 June 2024, term 7 Oct 2024 to 31 Aug 2025 (SGAE page). The nominee was Hervé Cassagnabère.
- No call was found for the Court of Justice seat itself; Gervasoni was a sitting General Court judge moved across. Only the nominee was published.
- No applicant count or shortlist was published for either.

### Germany — no advertisement at all

The Bundestag research service note of 2 Aug 2024 states the position directly: nomination is by the Bundesregierung with the consent of the Richterwahlausschuss (Richterwahlgesetz §1(3)), the committee votes in secret (§§11-12), and "Eine öffentliche Ausschreibung des Richterpostens erfolgt – anders als beispielsweise in Österreich – nicht." The 2024 Court of Justice renewal (von Danwitz) and the 2025 General Court renewals (Laitenberger, Steinfatt) therefore produced no call, no applicant data and no shortlist; only the names in the Council decisions.

### Italy — parliamentary information, not a public call

Law 234/2012 art. 17 requires the President of the Council or the Minister for European Affairs to inform the Chambers when the government proposes or designates Italian members of the Court of Justice, stating the procedure followed, the reasons for the choice and the CVs; the committees may hear the person after taking office, and the duty covers confirmations. No public call, applicant count or shortlist was located for Condinanzi and Biondi (2024) or Bestagno and Pezzuto (2025).

### Spain — public procedure exists, but renewals bypass it

Real Decreto 972/2020 provides for a BOE-published competitive call with a selection committee (arts. 3-4). Art. 8 creates a separate renewal route: the outgoing judge asks the Minister of Justice, who proposes to the Council of Ministers; a call follows only if renewal is refused. The 2025 General Court renewals of Martín y Pérez de Nanclares and Sampol Pucurull took that route, so no convocatoria and no candidate data exist for them. The last full call was Orden PCM/121/2021 (BOE 13 Feb 2021), which published criteria, a five-member committee and a 20-day deadline but still no applicant figures. There was no Court of Justice vacancy in the window.

### Ireland — public call for a contested seat, none for renewals

The June 2021 notice is the documented Irish procedure for a contested General Court seat: applications invited by 12 July 2021 in the Article 255 model CV format, an expert panel chaired by Nial Fennelly with the Attorney General and Professor Imelda Maher, interviews at the panel's discretion, and the nominee then interviewed by the Article 255 panel. Applicants were not named. The 2025 events were renewals of Kingston and Mac Eochaidh (decision of 2 Oct 2024, effective 1 Sept 2025); no application process was located for them.

### Belgium — nothing in the window

Belgium's last General Court appointments were proposed on 29 Oct 2021 (two judges) and its next Court of Justice proposal is dated 20 Mar 2026, both outside the window. The Council of Ministers communiqué format names the proposed person(s) only. The pages returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch, so this rests on the indexed title/date plus the absence of Belgium from all 2023-2025 appointment decisions.

## What this means for a production-versus-intake analysis

Three distinct national regimes, not one:

1. **Public call with a published shortlist** — Portugal only. Three names, parliamentary hearing, and the Article 255 rejection are all observable. This is the only state in the batch where an intake denominator (shortlist size, and the fact of a rejection) can be measured from official records.
2. **Public call with a confidential or unpublished pool** — Netherlands, Luxembourg, Austria, France. A vacancy and a deadline are public; the committee's recommendation, applicant count and ranking are not (the Netherlands says so explicitly). Intake can be dated but not sized.
3. **No public call** — Germany, Italy, Spain (renewals), Ireland (renewals), Belgium. Only the nominee, and in Italy the nominee's CV via the parliamentary informativa, becomes public.

Consequences for measurement:

- A per-state "applicants" series cannot be built for 2023-2025 in any of these ten states. Only Portugal yields a `candidates` value (3) and that is a shortlist, not an applicant count.
- Renewals are not competitions. In Spain (RD 972/2020 art. 8) and by practice elsewhere, an incumbent's renewal produces no pool at all. Counting renewals as "openings filled through competition" would inflate the intake denominator; the CSV keeps them separate and flags the route in the notes.
- The Article 255 panel is the only body that sees every nominee and every rejection across all ten states. Its activity reports should be used to cross-check the Portuguese rejection and to count negative opinions, since national records do not publish them.

- **Applicant counts**: none of the ten states publishes them for 2023-2025. Any figure of that kind in the database would be constructed, not observed.
