# National nomination records for CJEU/General Court judges — batch B (2023–2025)

Denmark, Finland, Sweden, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Poland, Czechia, Slovakia.
Scope: candidate lists, shortlists, application pools, and final nominees for the Court of
Justice (CoJ) and the General Court (EGC). Companion file: `data/probes/national_cjeu_b.csv`.
Sources saved under `sources/official/probes/national_cjeu_b/`.

The only genuine *pools* of applicants in this batch are Sweden's General Court case (9),
Poland's two 2023 competitions (6 and 7 admissible candidates) and Lithuania's General Court
competition (4 applicants). Everything else is a single-name nomination system, where the
absence of a pool is structural rather than a documentation gap.

## Category discipline

Four different quantities occur and are not interchangeable:

- **openings** — seats to be filled (from Council appointment decisions and national calls);
- **applicants** — persons who submitted an application in an open call (Sweden EGC, Lithuania EGC);
- **candidates** — persons who met the formal requirements and entered the competition
  (Poland; the published lists are explicitly "kandydatów … spełniających wymagania formalne",
  so they are a floor, not the number of applications received);
- **nominees** — persons formally nominated by the state (one per round in Denmark, Sweden,
  Czechia, Slovakia; two where two seats were filled).

No row sums across these categories.

## Country notes

### Denmark
Two seats, both triggered by resignation. Court of Justice: Lars Bay Larsen resigned with effect
7 Oct 2024; the government issued an open call (notice dated 18 Dec 2023, seen only as a Bar
Association reproduction of the government's text) and nominated **Niels Fenger**, then
Folketingets Ombudsman, on 19 Apr 2024; appointed 2 Oct 2024 for the remainder to 6 Oct 2027
(Decision (EU) 2024/2647). General Court: Sten Frimodt Nielsen resigned to become ambassador to
Indonesia; **Louise Spangsberg Grønfeldt** appointed 15 Sep 2023 to 31 Aug 2028.

`not_public` is structural: the *Dommerudnævnelsesrådet vedrørende internationale
dommerstillinger* recommends **one** candidate (§1(2) of its rules of procedure of 24 Jun 2019)
and its meetings are not public (§7). Denmark publishes a three-name list only for the ECHR post.

- https://www.justitsministeriet.dk/ministeriet/raad-naevn-og-udvalg/dommerudnaevnelsesraadet-vedroerende-internationale-dommerstillinger/ (council page, composition)

### Sweden
Court of Justice: the sitting judge did not seek renewal; Domarnämnden prepared the case from
late 2023, advertised in two daily papers and on websites, and the government nominated
**Fredrik Schalin** on 18 Apr 2024 (term 7 Oct 2024–6 Oct 2030). General Court: replacement for
Schalin to 31 Aug 2025 → **Jörgen Hettne**.

The EGC case is the batch's best-documented pool. Domarnämnden's protocol of 13 Nov 2024
(dnr 65-2024) lists nine applicants — Eva Edwardsson, Jörgen Hettne, Peter Lif, Johan Lindholm,
Jérôme Monsenego, Joakim Nergelius, Christina Olsen Lundh, Hans Sundberg, Ola Zetterquist —
interviewed five, and ranked three (Hettne, Edwardsson, Monsenego). The protocol is not on
`domstol.se` (protocols are supplied on request) and was read as an official-document copy hosted
by Lund University.

For the CoJ case no count is published. A Dagens Juridik article of 2 Apr 2024 says seven lawyers
applied; that is a news report and is **excluded** from the dataset pending the protocol.

- https://www.domstol.se/globalassets/filer/domstol/domarnamnden/arsredovisning-domarnamnden-2024.pdf (§3.4)
- https://www.aclu.lu.se/sites/aclu.lu.se/files/2024-12/11._Domarn%C3%A4mnden_13_november_2024.pdf

### Finland and Estonia — no procedure in the window
Every seat was occupied for a term extending past 2025, so no call was issued:

- Finland: Jääskinen (CoJ) renewed 7 Oct 2021 to 6 Oct 2027; Kanninen and Pynnä (EGC) to 31 Aug 2028.
- Estonia: Jürimäe (CoJ) to 6 Oct 2027; Madise and Nõmm (EGC) to 31 Aug 2028. The rotating
  advocate-general seat left Estonia when Priit Pikamäe's term ended on 6 Oct 2024; the successor
  is Lithuanian, so no Estonian nomination followed.

Both states do run open calls when a seat falls due (Finland: `ehdokasilmoittautuminen` →
preparatory council → government; Estonia: public competition run by the **Ministry of Foreign
Affairs** → commission → government → Riigikogu Constitutional Committee). The most recent
instances were 2020/2021 and fall outside the window.

`valtioneuvosto.fi` and `oikeusministerio.fi` are behind Cloudflare bot verification and return
HTTP 403; the 2020/2021 decisions were read from Wayback captures of the same official URLs.
A 2021 Finnish release naming Kanninen and Pynnä surfaced first in search results and was
discarded after dating (8 Jul 2021).

### Latvia
No competition. Both EGC seats fell due on 31 Aug 2025 and the Ministry of Justice forwarded the
two incumbents; the Cabinet approved **Pēteris Zilgalvis** (order 839) and **Inga Reine** (order 840)
on 15 Oct 2024. The TAP annotation states that alternative solutions were **not** assessed (`Nē`).
Latvia used a competition instrument in 2015 and 2018; it was not used here.

- https://www.vestnesis.lv/op/2024/202.15 · https://www.vestnesis.lv/op/2024/202.16
- https://likumi.lv/ta/id/355646

### Lithuania
General Court: call of 7 Aug 2024 for three posts (one to 31 Aug 2025 replacing Norkus, who became
advocate-general, and two full six-year terms from 1 Sep 2025). **Four candidates** submitted
documents; the working group (PM order No. 141 of 24 Jul 2024) published two lists on 18 Oct 2024;
the government proposed **Danutė Jočienė** and **Saulius Lukas Kalėda**, and the Seimas approved on
19 Dec 2024 (votes 101-1-1, 94-1-0, 98-0-0).

Court of Justice: no open call. The Seimas approved **Jarukaitis** for a second term and **Norkus**
as advocate-general on 28 Mar 2024 (105-0-2).

The "four candidates" figure is the only published count, and it is recorded as `partial`: the
ministry host (`tm.lrv.lt`) returns HTTP 403 to automated clients, has no Wayback capture of the
page, and the sentence was read from a university reprint quoting the ministry release.
`e-seimas.lrs.lt`, `lrv.lt`, `e-tar.lt` also return 403, so the Government resolution numbers
(1K-169/1K-170) are recorded as located-only.

- https://www.lrs.lt/sip/portal.show?p_k=1&p_r=35403&p_t=288014
- https://tm.lrv.lt/lt/naujienos-2/darbo-grupe-baige-kandidatu-i-esbt-teisejus-atranka/ (403 to bots)

### Poland
The single most transparent procedure in the batch, and the only one that ended with nothing
appointed. Two competitions were launched on 19 Jun 2023 (deadline 3 Jul 2023): one for the Court
of Justice seat vacant since Safjan's second term expired in 2021, one for the two General Court
seats vacant since Aug 2022.

- Published lists of candidates meeting formal requirements (3 Nov 2023): **6** for the CoJ —
  Bach-Golecka, Frąckowiak-Adamska, Koncewicz, Radwan, Wegner, Witkowski; **7** for the EGC —
  Bach-Golecka, Kowalik-Bańczyk, Półtorak, Radwan, Rutkowska, Susło, Szczepanik.
- Council of Ministers approved Bach-Golecka (CoJ) and Rutkowska and Radwan (EGC) on 7 Nov 2023;
  the Sejm's EU Affairs Committee gave its opinion on 9 Nov 2023.
- On 27 Dec 2023 the new government **withdrew** all three approvals, holding that the hearings
  were rushed (all in one day) and that the panel lacked EU-law expertise. No replacement
  competition was published in 2024–2025; the seats remained vacant.

### Czechia
No public call in the window; both decisions are renominations taken by the government on the
proposal of the ministers of justice and foreign affairs, after a selection commission:

- 16 Aug 2023, item 11, čj. 698/23 — nomination for the Court of Justice (term 7 Oct 2024–6 Oct 2030);
  the name, Jan Passer, follows from the Council reappointment of 8 Nov 2023, not from the item itself.
- 2 Oct 2024, item 22, čj. 765/24 — nomination of **two** General Court judges
  (Škvařilová-Pelzl and Petrlík), renewed 26 Mar 2025 for 1 Sep 2025–31 Aug 2031.

An open call is issued only for a vacant seat; both Czech EGC seats were continuously occupied.

- https://vlada.gov.cz/cz/vysledky-jednani-vlady-16-srpna-2023-207518/
- https://vlada.gov.cz/cz/media-centrum/aktualne/vysledky-jednani-vlady-2--rijna-2024-215749/

### Slovakia
Nomination-based under Art. 141a(6)(f) of the Constitution and §27a/§27g of Act 185/2002: the
Judicial Council issues a call for **nominations by entitled bodies** (not applications), elects by
secret ballot, and the government approves or refuses. One General Court seat took three rounds:

1. Andrej Stec — elected 15 Nov 2022, government approval 17 May 2023, **rejected by the Art. 255
   panel** on 7 Jul 2023.
2. Michal Kianička — sole nominee, elected 19 Sep 2023 (13-0-4), **refused by the government** on
   24 Apr 2024.
3. Juraj Schwarcz — sole nominee, elected 18 Jun 2024 (resolution 203/2024), government resolution
   **375/2024** of 26 Jun 2024, appointed by Council decision of 2 Oct 2024 to 31 Aug 2028.

Each round had exactly one nominee; there is no applicant pool to count. The Slovak Court of
Justice seat was not open (Gavalec to 6 Oct 2027); the next CoJ selection opened in May 2026.
