# College of Europe: production bounds

## Known

- 2024–25 enrolment: **496** students.
- Official VLUHR educational assessment (2017): **more than 90%** of students obtain the qualification within one year; about **75%** during the first exam session; drop-out is low.

Source:
`http://files.nvao.org/pdffiles/ACCR_3704_N6425_130241_2018-06-12_Master_of_Arts_in_EU_International_Relations_and_Diplomacy_Studies_rapport.pdf`

## Implication

Assuming the same completion regime, 496 enrolled students imply a rough production range of:

\[
496 \times 0.90 = 446 \quad\text{to}\quad 496
\]

This is a **bounded estimate**, not an observed graduate count. It should not be placed in `graduates` as if it were observed.

## Why it still does not give an overproduction ratio

The 2024–25 cohort cannot be compared with 2024 CJEU openings without a cohort lag. The College’s graduates enter the elite labour market years after graduation, and only a minority enter any given elite track. The missing numerator remains the **aspirant pool**, not merely the graduate count.

## What the source does establish

- Completion is high; enrolment is a reasonable upper-bound proxy for production.
- The College is not a high-dropout institution where enrolment and graduation diverge sharply.
- A sensitivity analysis can use 90–100% completion, explicitly labelled as an assumption.
