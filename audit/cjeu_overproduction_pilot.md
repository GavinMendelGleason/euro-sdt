# CJEU overproduction pilot

## Formal candidate pool

The Article 255 panel assesses proposed candidates. Its reports distinguish first-term candidates from renewals.

| Period | First-term candidates | Renewals | CJEU openings | Ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2019 | 21 | 6 | 19 | **1.11** |
| 2020–2022 | 29 | 24 | 26 | **1.12** |
| **Total** | **50** | **30** | **45** | **1.11** |

Openings are new entrants/replacements in the Court of Justice, Advocates General, and General Court, excluding renewals. The 2022 figure covers only January–February; the 2022 opening count covers the full year. This is a period aggregate, not a matched annual series.

Source:
- Sixth Activity Report (2019)
- Seventh Activity Report (2020–2022)

## Interpretation

Formal candidate competition was close to parity: **1.11 first-term candidates per opening**.

This does **not** show elite overproduction. The Article 255 pool is the set of candidates formally proposed by member states, not the wider pool of eligible judges, professors, and lawyers. Overproduction can occur upstream of nomination. It can also occur without a large formal candidate/opening ratio if the same candidates circulate across seats.

## What this does establish

- A defensible formal candidate denominator exists for 2019–2022.
- Candidate/opening ratios can be computed without imputation.
- Formal competition was not dramatically above one candidate per opening in this period.

## What remains

- 2023–2025 candidate-pool data; the eighth activity report was still in progress.
- The wider eligible pool, not just formal nominees.
- Annual matching of candidate opinions to openings.
- Whether failed candidates remain in the elite aspirant pool.
- Equivalent candidate-pool data for commissioners, DGs/DDGs, and corporate boards.
