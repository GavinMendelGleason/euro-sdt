# College of Europe graduate employment destinations: source-audited data for SDT analysis

Prepared: 4 November 2026  
Institution: **College of Europe**, Bruges/Natolin. Data about the University of Cologne from an earlier exchange are outside this report.  
Units: percentages are percentage points on a 0–100 scale; survey dates are observation dates, not graduation dates.

## 1. Findings and corrections

The verified College-wide employment snapshots are **August 2002, September 2016, April 2018, June 2019 and December 2020**. These are repeated cross-sections of recorded alumni careers, not annual placement rates for new graduates. A December 2024 snapshot covers one Bruges programme and is reported separately. Natolin also publishes annual first-position outcomes for the 2009–2018 graduating cohorts.

**This report supersedes the numerical tables in the preceding conversation.** Source images were checked against the legends, not inferred from the ordering of extracted PDF text.

- **2014: excluded.** The previously stated percentages could not be substantiated. The career pages checked in the 2016 and 2017 brochures contain lists of possible destinations without numerical distributions. Do not ingest the earlier 2014 row. [S09] [S10]
- **September 2016: corrected.** The source assigns 13% to international organisations/diplomacy, 10% to national/regional administration, 7% to academia/research/think tanks and 9% to others. The previous table misassigned these labels. [S02]
- **April 2018: corrected.** Academia is 8.79%, law firms 8.27%, national/regional public sector 12.53%, international organisations 5.56%, and NGOs/trade unions/other organisations 6.98%. Earlier figures misassigned several sectors. Consequently, the grouped business/consulting/law share is **35.14%**, not 35.66%. [S03]
- **2002: source inconsistency retained.** Its chart labels and printed counts do not agree exactly. Both are preserved below; count-derived percentages are the preferred representation of the numerical table. [S01]
- No verified 1990 employment-distribution observation was recovered. The 1999 material is contextual evidence, not a dated survey.

## 8. Instructions for the SDT analysis harness

### 8.1 Ingestion and provenance

1. Use this source-audited report rather than earlier chat tables. Preserve the source identifier with every value.
2. Preserve observation dates separately from publication years and graduation years. Set missing sample sizes to `NA`, never zero or the institution's total alumni count.
3. Keep College-wide snapshots, the programme sample, Natolin cohorts and historical narrative in separate datasets.
4. Keep raw labels alongside derived broad groups. Preserve the source's numerical precision; report changes as percentage points.
5. Prefer count-derived 2002 shares for analysis, while retaining the pie-label discrepancy as a quality flag. Do not fabricate narrower sectors from “Services.”
6. Permit the published 2018 sum of 100.01. Do not silently rescale, backfill, interpolate or extrapolate towards 1990.
7. Do not recover category counts by multiplying percentages by an assumed N. The later College-wide sample denominators are unknown.
8. Record the Natolin 802/794/796 discrepancy. Neither pooled column is an annual graduating class.

### 8.2 SDT interpretation and identification limits

Treat these data as evidence about the **allocation of careers among recorded graduates of one specialised institution**. They do not directly measure the production of elites, the supply of aspirants, access to power or frustrated elite ambitions.

For an analysis of credential production and recruitment bottlenecks:

- Define an elite position operationally. EU employment, a traineeship, a junior consultancy role and a senior decision-making office are distinct outcomes.
- Match annual graduate flows with entry-level vacancies, applications, appointments and competition success, rather than comparing the stock of all alumni with a single year's intake.
- Compare cohorts at fixed intervals after graduation and separate internships, temporary contracts and permanent jobs.
- Add graduating-cohort denominators, programme/campus mix, nationality and eligibility, prior employment and relevant labour-market changes.
- Test alternative accounts: changes in classification, coverage, cohort age, expansion of private EU-affairs work, and shifts in institutional recruitment.
- Distinguish observed descriptive patterns from explanations and causal estimates. Destination shares alone cannot establish institutional intent or elite overproduction.

### 8.3 Analyses supported now

- Describe broad recorded career allocation at each verified date.
- Run sensitivity comparisons with the native categories retained.
- Report Natolin first-position-type variation separately.
- Identify missing evidence required for an SDT mechanism.

### 8.4 Analyses not supported now

- A continuous 1990–2026 annual time series.
- An identified break around 2010.
- A causal effect of College attendance, selectivity or an elite strategy.
- Reliable annual permanent-EU-placement, unemployment or rejection rates.
- A population-wide conclusion about Europe's elite supply/demand balance.
