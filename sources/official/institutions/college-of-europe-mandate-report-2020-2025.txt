1. Study programmes: figures and achievements  1.1. Enrolment, applications and scholarships The College of Europe is looking back at five successfully implemented academic years across its Departments of European Economic Studies (ECO, Bruges), European Interdisciplinary Studies (EIS, Natolin), EU International Relations and Diplomacy Studies (IRD, Bruges), European Legal Studies (LAW, Bruges), European Political and Governance Studies (POL, Bruges) as well as the joint Master’s programme in Transatlantic Affairs (MATA, Bruges/Natolin, in cooperation with the Fletcher School at Tufts University in the U.S.) and the European General Studies Programme (EG, Bruges)1. With only minor deviations over the last years, the Bruges and the Natolin campuses consistently reach the target of their respective admission ceilings, with combined student enrolment in the range of 460 to 476 in the period 2020-21 to 2024-25. The academic year 2024-25, marking the 75th anniversary of the College of Europe, brought, with 496 students, a new enrolment record in the institution’s history. While the existing College programmes continued to operate at their maximum capacities, the first cohort of 32 students at the European Transformation and Integration Studies Department (ETI) in Tirana was part of the “promotion Jacques Delors”. The admission numbers for the academic year 2025-26 have confirmed an overall number close to 500 students. As regards the diversity of nationalities, the number of students’ declared citizenships lies now consistently above 50, showing the appeal of the College’s offer also beyond the EU member states. Administrative and organisational reforms 	2.1. A student-centred approach The administrative services of the College play a crucial role in the success of the College’s mission. Therefore, a spirit of continuous reform and improvement has been instilled in the administration of the Bruges campus. Against this backdrop, over the last years, a plan of new organisational structures has been developed and implemented along a student-centred approach. Notably, the Student Affairs Pillar has regrouped the Admissions Office, the newly created Student Welfare Office, as well as the Alumni and Careers Office under a single Director – overseeing the entire life cycle from applicant to alumni and constituting a bridge between students and administrative services.  The development of a comprehensive Code of Conduct by a working group consisting of students, faculty and staff in 2021 – whose implementation and revision has ever since been

overseen by a full-time Student Welfare Officer – has put a concrete and well-regarded policy around the College’s commitment to a zero-tolerance stance on discrimination, harassment and sexual misconduct. Over the recent years, the College has shown that it is ready to implement the policy in a fair but decisive manner. Furthermore, since 2021-22, the Bruges campus has conducted an anonymous survey each semester about its Code of Conduct. So far, all participants who had reported a breach of the Code of Conduct indicated their satisfaction with the handling of the proceedings. Complementing the growing number and highly appreciated student initiatives in this regard, the Student Welfare Officer also takes up the role of Diversity and Inclusion Advisor – initiating also own projects on the topic from and within the administration. The decision to significantly increase the funds for external psychological and mental health sessions on campus, especially in response to an increased demand after the Covid-19 pandemic (see section 5), has proven right. A holistic approach to student welfare has been put into place, also drawing on programmes by the Flemish Government to extend the offer. Students facing difficulties during their academic year receive immediate and unbureaucratic support – via the Hardship Fund (relaunched in 2021), also for financial problems. The students’ appreciation of the Alumni and Careers Office has significantly increased since the reforms in 2021-22. Under a full-time officer, the offer has been completely revised and extended. The links and cooperation with the College’s Alumni Association have become closer. The development of the Alumni and Careers Office continues as we recently signed a partnership with Amazon to develop, over the next three years, a skills and language centre. The skills and language centre is a key initiative that will allow us to strengthen our soft skills and language offer, to enrich the academic experience of our students with professional skills, allowing their smooth integration on the job market.  Most of these reforms have been informed by the Rector’s personal exchanges with students, which are a core element of her weekly schedule. In addition to guest lectures in courses, participation in extracurricular events and contributions to student activities, since 2021, Rector Mogherini has introduced student office hours. During these hours, the Rector is available to discuss any topic – academic, administrative or extra-curricular one-to-one with our students. These hours, which allowed hundreds of students to share their experiences and feedback, are a constant inspiration to improve and bring innovation to the functioning of the College.  Independent of the reforms and relating to the core of what the communities in both Bruges and Natolin stand for, the College of Europe offered, among other efforts, accommodation to Ukrainian alumni fleeing the country after the Russian full-scale invasion in February 2022. Also, the crisis in the Middle East since October 2023 has been addressed in a community-based approach, with events and open exchanges including students and staff.

European Union Diplomatic Academy  The need for the creation of a European Diplomatic Academy, which brings together diplomats from member states to train them also as EU diplomats, has been raised in both academic and political circles for more than 20 years. However, even after the establishment of the European External Action Service (EEAS) in 2011, these ideas would remain shelved for many more years. Based on an initiative for a pilot project of a European Diplomatic Academy, originating from the European Parliament in 2021 and carried forward by the EEAS in 2022, the College of Europe in Bruges and Natolin took on the challenge to move a promising idea via a successful implementation to its long-term consolidation. Having won the tender procedure for the first pilot year 2022-23 in May 2022, a cohort of 40 junior diplomats from EU member states and candidate countries, as well as civil servants from the EU institutions, started their journey with the Natolin study trip module of two weeks already in late August 2022, before continuing the 9-month programme on the Bruges campus.  Carried by the enthusiasm of the first cohort, the College also won the tender for the second pilot 2023-24, split into two editions of 5 months each, maintaining the highly successful elements of the first year (like the module in Natolin and the regular excursions to Brussels) and integrating new components like a visit to Luxembourg and Strasbourg as well as a one-month job placement at the EEAS. Diplomats from candidate countries were not included in the EEAS’ proposal for the second pilot year. With 51 participants in total for the two editions of the second pilot programme, the European Diplomatic Academy alumni network had grown to more than 90 at the end of the two pilot years. Against the backdrop of the successful implementation of the pilot phase, EU member states established the long-term, fully fledged European Union Diplomatic Academy (EUDA) in Council Decision (CFSP) 2024/1472 of 21 May 2024 – granting the implementation to the College of Europe. Following the general structure of the second pilot year, the first cohorts under the permanent setup started their programme in August 2024 (15 participants) and February 2025 (28 participants), respectively. The European Union Diplomatic Programme for the Enlargement Region (EUDP), a track in parallel to EUDA, for up to 15 junior diplomats from candidate countries, to live and learn together with their peers from EU member states, started in February 2025. The EUDA is financed by the European Commission’s DG ENEST.  Building an “esprit de corps” in line with the College’s philosophy, EUDA and EUDP participants stay at the Spanjaard residence (49 rooms), acquired by the Bruges campus in 2022 and serving also as the place of accommodation for the visiting professors.  Numerous synergies have been created between the EUDA/EUDP and the College’s academic life, student initiatives and executive education, further fostering the inclusion of the EUDA/EUDP participants in the College community.

5. Navigating challenges: Covid-19 pandemic and inflation  5.1. The Covid years 2020-2022 The start of Rector Mogherini’s mandate fell into the probably most difficult period for academic and student life in the College of Europe’s history. Maintaining the College formula of “living and studying together” in the times of social distancing, lockdowns, regular testing, and compulsory telework has been a challenge in all dimensions.  Especially for the Mário Soares Promotion of 2020-21, both in Bruges and in Natolin, a decision to have students leave campus and follow their programmes remotely from their respective homes would have been, on the implementation side, a relatively easy route to choose. In agreement with all decision-making bodies, the College made the strategic choice, however, to face the daily challenges of holding up, also for this cohort, the residential format and, thereby, not deprive students of their once-in-a-lifetime European experience. Hence, under immense pressure, adaptations to the set-up of all dimensions of the College life were made in order to respond to the sanitary situation adequately (in line with the respective regulations in Belgium and Poland) and provide the safest possible environment for the community – while, at the same time, giving the students, wherever and whenever possible, at least approximate access to what the College has to offer in ‘normal’ years.  This approach required the establishment and constant refinement of teaching via video conferencing and active online engagement. To maintain the academic standards also in students’ assessment, and beyond the technical solutions, the Academic Council developed an entirely new set of rules and procedures. On the student affairs side, building on the joint efforts of numerous services and especially the Health and Safety Committee (in Bruges), the administration around the health measures and active support of and guidance for students (including mental health) have been the number one priority. Next to special classroom setups, and among many other adaptations, an online reservation system and visitors’ counter were introduced in the Library to ensure the best possible study conditions under the varying degree of restrictions. The College’s actions also included sanctions against students disrespecting the rules and moderating the relations with the broader local community in this regard. That the alumni of the Covid-19 years, and especially those of the Mário Soares Promotion, overwhelmingly state that they managed to build their very own “esprit du Collège” in extraordinary social and academic circumstances, is the biggest recognition for each and every one who went beyond themselves, making their contribution to the College’s successful navigation of the pandemic.   5.2. The restructuring process was built on the premise of minimising the impact on the academic and student life dimensions of the campus and therefore focused on the reduction of staff in the administration. The termination of some employment contracts, among other measures to address structural deficits, built the basis for a significant rebound in 2023 and 2024. Institutional outreach, high-level events and partnerships  6.1 Events and outreach While the establishment of the Tirana campus and the EUDA have drawn, as milestone projects, a lot of attention to the College of Europe, increasing also the overall institutional outreach and consolidating the institution as a hub for partnerships and high-level events has been a general priority over the last years. Embedded in the local community, the Bruges campus’s relations with its city are of utmost importance. The continuation of valuable traditions over the last years, including the host family project and the student welcome and farewell drinks with the Mayor, has been complemented with active links and contributions fostered around key projects like EUDA.  The traditional Opening Ceremony of the academic year in the Bruges Concert Hall has – since 2021 – been mirrored by an upgraded Closing Ceremony in the same location, welcoming equally high-level speakers. Also, during the flow of the academic year, the College of Europe established itself as a platform for events and speeches of the highest level. These events have undoubtedly broadened the students’ perspective on what is at stake in the fight for freedom and democracy.
