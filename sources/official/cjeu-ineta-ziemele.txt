Ineta Ziemele
Judge
Born in 1970 in Jelgava (Latvia), Ineta Ziemele obtained a law degree from the Latvijas Universitāte (University of Latvia) in 1993, which she supplemented that year with postgraduate studies in the American legal system, the law and politics of the European Communities and political science at the Aarhus Universitet (Aarhus University, Denmark). She went on to obtain a Masters in international law (M.I.L.) at the Lunds Universitet (Lund University, Sweden) in 1994 and completed her studies at the University of Cambridge (United Kingdom), where she obtained a PhD in law in 1999.
She began her professional career as a parliamentary assistant at the Latvian Parliament from 1990 to 1992, before working as a consultant to the Foreign Affairs committee of the Republic of Latvia from 1992 to 1995. In 1995, she was appointed as an adviser to the Prime Minister of Latvia in matters of international and European law. From 1999 to 2001, she was a Programme Adviser at the Directorate-General of Human Rights of the Council of Europe in Strasbourg (France).
In addition, Ms Ziemele taught from 1993 as a university assistant in the Legal and Political Theory Department and the International Law and Law of the Sea Department of the Latvijas Universitāte. She went on to work as a lecturer in International law and European law until 1999. In 1995, she founded the Human Rights Institute at the Latvijas Universitāte, of which she was the director until 1999. Subsequently, she was appointed the ‘Söderberg’ Professor of International Law and Human Rights at the Rīgas Juridiskā augstskola (Riga Graduate School of Law, Latvia), from 2001 to 2005. During the same period, she also taught at the Raoul Wallenberg Institute at Lunds Universitet as a visiting professor. In 2001, she founded the ‘Baltic Yearbook of International Law’, of which she remains the Co-Editor-in-Chief. Since 2009, she has been a professor at the Rīgas Juridiskā augstskola (Riga Graduate School of Law, Latvia).
Ms Ziemele’s career in the judiciary began in 2005 with her appointment as a judge at the European Court of Human Rights in Strasbourg (France), where she also held the position of President of Chamber from 2012 to 2014. In 2015 she was appointed as a judge at the Latvijas Republikas Satversmes tiesa (Constitutional Court, Latvia) of which she served as President from 2017 to 2020.
In 2025 she was elected as a full member of the Latvian Academy of Sciences. Her legal research has led to numerous publications in the fields of international law, human rights law, and Latvian and European constitutional law.
Ms Ziemele was appointed as a judge at the Court of Justice on 6 October 2020, and has served in that capacity since then.

Jan Passer Judge Biography and career Born in 1974 in Prague (Czechoslovakia), Mr Jan Passer studied at the Universita Karlova (Charles University, Czech Republic) where he obtained his law degree. He also completed a Masters of Laws at the Stockholms universitet (University of Stockholm, Sweden) in 2000. In 2007, he defended his doctoral thesis in law at the Universita Karlova. Since 1997, he carried out a number of judicial functionsin his home country, first as a trainee judge at the Městský soud v Praze (Prague City Court, Czech Republic), a position which he held until 2001, and then as a judge at the Obvodní soud pro Prahu 2 (Prague 2 District Court, Czech Republic), from 2001 to 2005. From 2005 to 2016, he was a judge at the Nejvyšší správní soud (Supreme Administrative Court, Czech Republic). The substantial experience gained during his practice took Mr Passer to teaching. He was a lecturer in, amongst other subjects, EU law at the Universita Karlova from 2001 to 2003, at the Masarykova univerzita (Masaryk University, Czech Republic) from 2006 to 2016, and at the Univerzita Palackého v Olomouci (Palacký University Olomouc, Czech Republic) from 2014 to 2016. That expertise also led him to teach law at the Judicial Academy of the Czech Republic from 2001 to 2016. Mr Passer was appointed as a Judge at the General Court of the European Union on 19 September 2016. He served at the General Court for four years before being appointed, on 6 October 2020, to his current role as a Judge at the Court of Justice.
