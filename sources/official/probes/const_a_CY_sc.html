Supreme Court

Home Page / Judicial Service / Court Structure / Supreme Court


The Supreme Court consists of a number of judges not exceeding seven, pursuant to Article 3(8)(c) of the Administration of Justice (Miscellaneous Provisions) Law, Law 33/64, one of whom is the President of the Court. The Supreme Court exercises, inter alia, the following jurisdictions:

(a) It exercises the jurisdiction and powers vested in the Supreme Court by the Constitution, including the exclusive jurisdiction to issue the prerogative writs of Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Certiorari, Quo Warranto and Prohibition.

(b) It adjudicates upon appeals referred to it by the Court of Appeal against decisions of courts exercising civil and criminal jurisdiction, including courts of special jurisdiction, on matters of major public interest or general public importance, or for the consistency of the law in cases involving conflicting or contradictory decisions of the Court of Appeal.

(c) It determines, at a third and final level, upon an application submitted by the Attorney General of the Republic or by any of the parties, following leave granted by the Court itself and after prior civil or criminal appellate proceedings, legal issues arising from the decision of the Court of Appeal and connected with the deviation from established case law, or with the need for the correct interpretation of either primary or secondary substantive legislative provisions, or involving a major issue of public interest or general public importance, or the consistency of the law in cases of conflicting or contradictory decisions of the Court of Appeal in the exercise of its civil or criminal jurisdiction.

(d) It decides on the retrial by the Court of Appeal or by the court of first-instance criminal jurisdiction, as the case may be, of a criminal case in which a conviction was issued, either at first instance and conclusively or on appeal, on the basis of new evidence or facts which, in its judgment, may potentially overturn, in whole or in part, the decision.

(e) It hears applications for the recusal of a judge of any other court, except the Supreme Constitutional Court, the Administrative Court of Appeal and the Administrative Courts, following the dismissal of such recusal application by the competent court in each case.

(f) It hears cases of second-instance civil jurisdiction that were filed before 31/12/2017.

(g) It exercises first-instance admiralty jurisdiction until the commencement of the operation of the Admiralty Court pursuant to Article 33(2) of the Commercial Court and Admiralty Court Establishment and Operation Law of 2022 (69(I)/2022), as well as second-instance admiralty jurisdiction, which upon the commencement of the operation of the Admiralty Court shall thereafter be exercised by the Court of Appeal.

The Supreme Court is seated in Nicosia and is located on Charalambou Mouskou Street.
