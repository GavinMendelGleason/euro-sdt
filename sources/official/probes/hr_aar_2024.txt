DG HR IN BRIEF
In line with Commission’s corporate Human Resources (HR) strategy, DG HR aims at
modernising the Commission and creating the right conditions for staff to deliver on the
Commission’s priorities (1).

DG HR’s values are as follows, and consist of providing:
• Honest, fair, sustainable and ethical practice.
• Efficient, client-centric, high-quality human resources (HR) services.
• A positive, inclusive and respectful work environment.
• A flexible work environment that encourages teamwork, collaboration and innovation and
   promotes personal and professional development.
• Strengthened management capability across the Commission and a culture of high
   individual and team performance.
• An enshrined security culture.

In pursuing its mission, DG HR carries out the following main types of activities:
• ensuring a safe, green and healthy work environment where staff’s physical and mental
    well-being is supported;
• supporting new, flexible ways of working with a focus on trust-based management;
• building a diverse, inclusive and accessible organisation;
• delivering efficient, staff-focused HR services supporting staff in all stages of their career;
• setting the strategic HR framework within an effective and efficient use of resources;
• adopting a forward-looking approach to ensure the Commission has the right skills to
    deliver on its priorities now and in the future;
• ensuring the safety, security and protection of staff, property, activities and information
    at all places of employment; and
• ensure full respect of the Staff Regulations and open and efficient social dialogue with
    staff representatives to guarantee adequate working conditions.

The legal framework regarding EU staff matters comprises the Treaty on the functioning
of the European Union (TFEU), the Staff regulations of Officials (SR) and Conditions of
Employment of Other Servants of the European Union (CEOS), delegated acts, EU directives,
case law and report on the implementation of the Staff Regulations by all institutions.
Sysper is the main HR management information system supporting most HR processes
under the responsibility of DG HR. Work is however ongoing to develop a new user-friendly
HR IT platform which will replace existing systems. In 2024, DG HR counted 1 330 statutory
staff members (2). DG HR implemented its budget of EUR 418,29 million under centralised
direct management. On 1 May 2024, Stephen Quest became the new Director-General.

Resources and Security (DG HR) to the College of Commissioners. Annual activity reports

constitute the basis on which the College takes political responsibility for the decisions it
takes as well as for the coordinating, executive and management functions it exercises, as

interest of all Europeans by offering a modern workplace and rewarding careers that
attract top talent from all Member States. DG HR also progressed working toward the vision
set out in the HR strategy in which all staff in the Commission work in a more trust-based,
people-focused environment and following modern and flexible work practice.

C. Key conclusions on internal control and financial management

DG HR has systematically examined the available control results and indicators, as well as
the observations and recommendations issued by the Internal Audit Service (IAS) and the
European Court of Auditors (ECA). These elements have been assessed to determine their
impact on management's assurance about the achievement of the control objectives.
Please refer to Section 2 for further details.

In conclusion, management has reasonable assurance that, overall, suitable controls are in
place and working as intended; risks are being appropriately monitored and mitigated.
Improvements are necessary in the areas of physical security of persons and assets in the
Commission, the protection of confidentiality of information at corporate level and the
assessment of HR needs in the Commission at corporate level. These result from key
recommendations issued by the IAS in these specific areas. Mitigating actions have been
implemented and continue to be reinforced, as detailed in Section 2.2. The Director-General,
in his capacity as Authorising Officer by Delegation, has signed the Declaration of
Assurance.

1.1.1. An attractive workplace
Ethical conduct is at the heart of the professionalism of Commission staff and ethics and
integrity are an integral part of our organisational culture. In 2024, DG HR expanded its
awareness-raising activities with a view to enhancing staff members’ knowledge of and
compliance with their ethical obligations. Notably, DG HR organised more than 80 training
sessions (up from 63 in 2023) targeting staff at all levels, including senior and middle
managers and tailor-made training for services with higher risks. The number of requests
for authorisations also grew substantially (for example for outside activities, and spouse
employment), pointing to an enhanced awareness of ethical obligations. Moreover, the
Commission continued to lead regular interinstitutional exchanges on ethical matters with
representatives of all other EU institutions and bodies. This has been crucial in helping the
Institution to further enhance the robustness of its ethical framework and to share and
learn from best practice from across the EU institutions.

technological changes and varying levels of digital literacy among staff has impacted

Commission objectives by creating a flexible and efficient working environment that aligns
with the principles of modern workplace management.

The Flexible Working Change team, consisting of staff members from DG HR, the Office
for Infrastructure and Logistics in Brussels (OIB), the Office for Infrastructure and Logistics
in Luxembourg (OIL), DG for Digital Services (DIGIT), DG Interpretation (SCIC), the

management support. It included workshops, presentations, training (lunchtime
conferences), tailored advice, hands-on support during the moves, and post-move
evaluation exercises (100-day stocktaking). This was supported by complementary
measures such as open days, guided visits and communication through corporate channels
to all staff. As this is a rolling programme of support, first steps have been taken in the
process to accompany the next DGs that will move in 2025 and 2026. Additionally, work
was undertaken to understand trends, successes and challenges related to the flexible
workplace, notably by an in-depth analysis of the 2023 staff survey results relating to
flexible working, and through a dedicated pulse survey run in November 2024 on flexible
working in flexible offices.

1.1.2. Ensuring staff well-being and satisfaction
DG HR has supported the Commission’s general objective through various efforts by
fostering a healthy, adaptable, and inclusive workplace, ultimately contributing to higher
levels of staff satisfaction and improved performance. In 2024, DG HR continued to
advance the health and well-being strategy, navigating both internal and external
challenges. DG HR coordinated and implemented actions focused on physical and mental
well-being, both in office and remote work settings. Key results include:

   •   Mental well-being culture promotion: DG HR launched awareness campaigns
       and training sessions aimed at destigmatising mental health issues. This included
       workshops on resilience, stress management, and mindfulness practice as well as
       continuing the training of Mental Health First Aiders holding 32 courses for 250
       participants. DG HR has also launched Be Well Telecare, a telephone service which
       offers a first point of contact to staff who may be experiencing loneliness, anxiety,
       stress or other mental health issue at work.
   •   Absence/Sickness Management Enhancements: Further guidance has been
       published to better support the reintegration process at work after long-term sick
       leave. A new decision on absence and sickness management has been drafted,
       proposing standard procedures and simplification. The draft decision is currently
       being discussed internally, before further validation, consultation and adoption
       planned for 2025.
   •   Inclusive Health Initiatives: Targeted programmes and campaigns (e.g. Mental
       Health Week, Self-Care days and BeWell@My workplace, Fitness Days) addressed
       the diverse needs of staff in Brussels, Luxembourg and on JRC sites. Initiatives
       included ergonomic assessments and fitness challenges.

In 2024, DG HR continued to work on reinforcing its health and safety management
system to contribute to the well-being of staff, based on a gap analysis by reference to
the requirements of ISO 45001, the international standard for occupational health and
safety management systems. DG HR relaunched actions to raise managerial awareness of
the prevention of psycho-social risks. DG HR finalised and started implementing an action
plan on first intervention (fire prevention, evacuations and first aid), together with OIB and
OIL, considering new flexible working conditions. DG HR also prepared a global annual
report on health and safety, harmonised across all sites.

2.1.4. Economy of controls
In 2024, DG HR estimated the costs of controls at around EUR 11.4 million, remaining
broadly in line with 2023 (EUR 11.8 million). This represents 2.7% of the total payments,
slightly down from 3.1% in 2023. The estimation is based on the cost of human resources
dedicated to key financial control activities.

The cost of ex ante financial controls alone is estimated at around EUR 11.3 million,
corresponding to 2.7% of total payments, while ex post financial controls account for 0.1%
of verified transactions, amounting to around EUR 87 000.

Although the European Schools account for a large share of DG HR’s expenditure, over 99%
of all transactions concern other financial operations under the HRM support services. These
transactions, while generally of low to medium value, require extensive ex ante checks in
line with Financial Regulation and public procurement provisions. As a result, further cost
reductions would jeopardize sound financial management, leading to risks that would

3.1.    Human resource management
DG HR continued to actively implement the Commission HR strategy, while continuing to
work towards its own target of returning 150 FTEs by the end of 2027. By 31 December
2024, a total of 100 FTEs had been returned to the central pool, i.e. two thirds of the
target.

A management seminar was dedicated to putting a new AI tool into practice and in the area
of well-being, ‘help the helper’ pilot training was offered to DG HR staff to better handle
and prevent empathic distress fatigue.

The results of the 2023 staff survey provided an opportunity to take stock of key
satisfaction indicators, including staff well-being. Overall, DG HR had very encouraging
results: the participation rate increased with 19 percentage points and there was a record
level of staff engagement (78%). In general, DG HR’s averages were higher than those of
the Commission as a whole and there were improvements in 15 of the 16 themes of the
staff survey. Despite the challenges the DG has faced with implementing the new HR
service model and the reduction of posts, there were excellent results in the field of
teamwork and colleagues (90%).

In the follow-up to the staff survey, four major themes were identified: 1) well-being and
work-life balance/working arrangements; 2) collaboration and communication; 3) senior
management team; and 4) change and innovation. Staff-led working groups identified
actions that will be implemented in 2025.

Other examples of staff engagement actions taken in 2024, were the launch of the
monthly all-staff ʼQuestion Hourʼ session (led by the Director-General) and the
continuation of the DG HR Sounding Board launched in 2023, where a selected group of
staff provide their insights and opinions on HR matters relating to DG HR staff. 2024 also
saw the transition to a new local intranet on the SharePoint online platform, and the use of
Viva Engage, around the theme of ʼWe are DG HRʼ.

At the end of 2024, 41% of middle managers were women. An improved balance is
expected in 2025 when a number of currently vacant positions will be filled. DG HR also
continued to make additional effort to enlarging its pool of potential female managers. It
did so by appointing five women to Deputy Head of Unit positions, (bringing the total to
53%) and by encouraging women to follow the Female Talent Development Programme.
The percentage of female Deputies to Directors remained stable at 43%.

Also, in the field of diversity, DG HR continued its efforts to improve geographical balance
by implementing corporate measures.

3.2.    Digital transformation and information management
In 2024, DG HR continued rolling out the Commission’s new HR IT platform as an enabler of
the HR strategy. The HRT programme applies the principle and goals of the corporate digital
strategy as well as corporate architectural principles, such as cloud security. It stretches
over seven years and will ensure full integration of HR service delivery to the new single
platform ServiceNow.

The HRT programme is supported by several cross-cutting activities/themes such as data
protection, IT security, digital architecture, and data governance. Regarding data
architecture and data governance, the support for HRT focused on designing a business-
based data model to ensure a stable and comprehensive implementation of HR business
processes. The data model is built iteratively, aligned with the progress of individual HRT
projects. Additionally, attention has been given to clarify and align business concepts and
terms, across the HR Family. This activity enables a common implementation of a given
business concept in multiple IT systems and enables integration and data reuse between IT
systems and complies with the corporate 'once-only' data entry principle.

DG HR continued its effort in making progress on IT security plans, compliance attestations,
multi-factor authentication and EU Login, as well as security assurance activities. This
means that almost all information systems are covered by an up-to-date IT security plan,
and almost all IT security controls have compliance attestation. Nearly all information
systems are compliant with the ITCB decision regarding the use of multi-factor
authentication and EU Login.

In 2024, DG HR continued to manage its information and knowledge better and to
implement more collaborative working methods. This was done especially by individual and
group coaching to middle and senior managers as key enablers to support the adoption of
M365 tools and new ways of working.

To ensure the preparedness of staff to use the new platform in ServiceNow, many training
sessions were provided to the case handlers, in particular to the time management case
handlers and gradually to the various HR units.

With regard to the implementation of the Commission's Data Protection Action Plan, DG HR
continued its efforts to raise awareness on data protection within DG HR. 70 staff members
took part in data protection training during the year. Some 1 759 DG HR staff members
participated in this training over the 2019-2024 period (out of 1 293 HR staff members -
some participated more than once).

DG HR has continued to ensure that its processing operations are appropriately documented
in data protection records, which are continuously reviewed and kept up to date on a bi-
annual basis. DG HR has established agreements with external processors that process
personal data on behalf of DG HR. In 2024, it replied to 37 requests from data subjects to
exercise their data protection rights. DG HR’s data protection coordinators delivered written
data protection advice in 450 instances and provided 285 oral consultations.

The EMAS coordination team in DG HR coordinates the Commission Eco-Management and

       practice;
   •   Opening some floors of L107 to use as a working ‘hub’ for colleagues choosing to
