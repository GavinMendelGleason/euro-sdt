MESSAGE FROM THE


President
                          Katerina Stamatiou

                          President
                          of the Supreme Court



                          Welcome to the first Annual Review of the Supreme Court (“the Court”) since its
                          division in July 2023. Our aim is to present to you the work of the Court as well as its
                          main achievements over the past year.

                          Undoubtably, the independence of the judiciary, the quality of justice and its efficient
                          administration are fundamental to the rule of law. The judicial branch in its role as one
                          of the three powers of the State and the guardian of fundamental rights and freedoms
                          of citizens, pursues, at all times, the fair, impartial, and timely administration of justice. In
                          our efforts to address the chronic court backlog, we embarked upon the largest reform
                          programme ever attempted in the history of the Republic of Cyprus. Indeed, a very
                          complex, and multi-levelled project.

                                      Since the 1st of July 2023, the judicial map of Cyprus has changed. With the coming
                                      into force of the Reform Laws, the Supreme Court of thirteen Justices, was divided into
                                      a Supreme Constitutional Court and a Supreme Court, consisting of nine and seven
                                      Justices, respectively. The Supreme Court is now a third instance court with jurisdiction to
                                      hear criminal and civil cases. Its competence and jurisdictions are laid down in section 9(3)
                                      of the Administration of Justice Law of 1964, L. 33/1964, as amended, and are exercised
                                      only with the leave of the Court. At the same time, it adjudicates, at first and second
                                      instance, on all prerogative orders of Article 155.4 of the Constitution of the Republic.
                                      What’s more, the Supreme Court has undertaken the responsibility of determining all civil
                                      appeals lodged by the 31.12.2017, in our efforts to eliminate the backlog of civil appeals.

                                      The Supreme Court is responsible for supervising all courts of the Republic and, sits, as
                                      the Supreme Council of Judicature (“SCJ”) by virtue of Article 157 of the Constitution.
                                      Aiming to enhance transparency in judicial appointments and promotions, the SCJ’s
                                      composition has been enlarged with the participation of the Attorney-General or Deputy
                                      Attorney-General, the President or Vice-President of the Cyprus Bar Association and two
                                      jurists of a high professional and moral standard. The said participants hold, however, no
                                      voting rights. The Supreme Court is also competent to act as a Council for the retirement,
                                      dismissal or any matter that relates to the Justices of the Supreme Constitutional Court.

MESSAGE FROM THE


Chief Registrar
                          Irene Christodoulou

                          Chief Registrar
                          Supreme Court


                          The year 2024 was a defining one for the judicial system of Cyprus, marked by the
                          implementation of critical reforms aimed at improving the administration of justice.
                          At the heart of these changes was the separation of the former Supreme Court into
                          the Supreme Constitutional Court and the Supreme Court. Equally significant was
                          the simultaneous establishment of the Court of Appeal, which now exercises second-
                          instance jurisdiction. This development marked the creation of a three-tier jurisdiction in
                          the Cypriot legal system, enhancing the judicial process at all levels.

                          This institutional restructuring was primarily designed to address the growing backlog
                          of cases, particularly at the appellate level, which had surged in recent years. The delays
                          in adjudicating cases had caused significant dysfunction within the system, adversely
                          impacting the efficiency of court proceedings. The new structure seeks to alleviate
                          congestion and improve the speed and effectiveness of the administration of justice.

                          In early 2024, an attempt was made to transition to the electronic e-justice system,
                          intended to replace the temporary i-justice system. However, this effort was
                          unsuccessful, and the “temporary” system continues to serve the needs of the courts.
                          This highlighted the challenges associated with technological upgrades, including the
                          need for adequate infrastructure, specialized personnel, and effective user training.
                          Despite the setback, technology remains a critical component of judicial modernization,
                          with plans for the future implementation of a comprehensive court case management
                          information system still underway.

                          The new Civil Procedure Rules entered into force on September 1, 2023, with the
                          objective of modernizing civil litigation and providing citizens with quicker and more
                          effective judicial proceedings. These Rules are founded on the principle of “overriding
                          objective,” which grants courts enhanced powers to actively manage cases, while
                          imposing an obligation on the parties to cooperate in achieving the timely resolution of
                          disputes.

                          The introduction of the new Rules increases flexibility and efficiency in case
                          management, promoting the swift administration of justice. Their implementation is
                          expected to create a more efficient and user-friendly judicial system, better aligned with
                          society’s needs and citizens’ expectations.

                       Throughout 2024, extensive meetings were conducted among various government
                       bodies to discuss and advance the final stage of the judicial reform, focusing on the
                       implementation of the “Court Service” project. These discussions concluded in the
                       development of a preliminary proposal aimed at restructuring, organizing, and enhancing
                       the administrative functioning of the courts.

                       This proposal was submitted to the Supreme Constitutional Court, the Supreme Court,
                       and all relevant government bodies to foster broader consensus. The implementation of
                       this project is anticipated to enhance coordination, transparency, and efficiency within
                       the Judicial Service, making it more flexible and adaptable to contemporary needs.

                       Court and registry staff played a vital role in ensuring the smooth operation of the
                       courts amidst these reforms. Through their dedication and professionalism, they
                       supported the transition, managed the increased workload, and addressed the
                       challenges brought by new procedural and technological standards. Their contribution
                       has been instrumental not only in facilitating the adjustment to these changes but also in
                       maintaining public confidence in the Judicial Service despite the difficulties encountered.
                       However, there remains significant scope for improvement, particularly in the areas of
                       continuous staff training, strengthening human resources, and providing modern tools to
                       enhance efficiency and meet the growing demands of contemporary times.

                       The appraisal for the year 2024, despite the challenges and difficulties faced, highlights
                       the transformative impact of these changes and the progress achieved. These reforms
                       represent a significant step towards a justice system that effectively addresses the
                       needs of the people, reinforces trust in the judiciary, and upholds the fundamental value
                       of Justice.

                             The Cyprus Judicial Training School plays a pivotal role in promoting justice by providing continuous
                             training, primarily to Judges as well as to court staff, thus enhancing their skills and expertise in crucial
                             legal matters. Through its role in delivering training, it ensures that Judges maintain a robust and evolving
                             professional foundation capable of meeting the demands of modern justice. Additionally, systematic training
                             strengthens judicial knowledge whilst concretely contributing to the preservation and enhancement of
                             judicial independence.

                             In 2024, the School organized a series of significant training activities, aimed at enhancing and promoting
                             new knowledge and working methods as well as judicial skills, thus substantially contributing to judicial
                             practice. A total of thirteen seminars were delivered, two of which were organised in collaboration with
                             international organizations, specifically the European Union Agency for Asylum (EUAA) and the Embassy of
                             the United States of America. The School also collaborated with esteemed judges such as Mr. Konstantinos
                             Lykourgos, Judge and President of the Third Chamber at the Court of Justice of the European Union, and
                             Ms. Stavroula Ktistaki, Counselor of State and professor at the Department of Public Administration of
                             Panteion University.


SUPREME COURT OF CYPRUS                                                                                                                14
In light of their specialized topics and added value to the participants, the following seminars are particularly noteworthy:

Seminar: “Judicial Workshop on Evidence and Credibility Assessment” (July 4-5)
This seminar was organized in collaboration with the European Union Agency for Asylum (EUAA), focusing on the evaluation of
evidence in asylum cases, particularly regarding medical reports and documents originating from third countries. The judicial
trainers from Ireland and Greece presented methods for assessing the credibility of documents whilst taking into consideration
cultural and jurisdictional differences. The seminar was tailored for judges of the Court of Appeal (Administrative Bench) and
the Administrative Court for International Protection, highlighting the challenges of delivering justice in complex international
protection cases.



Seminar: “U.S. Sentencing Guidelines and Plea Bargaining” (September 19)
In the framework of our ongoing collaboration with the Embassy of the United States of America and the U.S. Department of
Justice, the School co-organized the above-mentioned seminar, which focused on the application of the Sentencing Guidelines
and the plea bargaining process in the United States. The first part of the seminar provided an overview of the U.S. sentencing
framework, followed by a practical exercise using real-life examples. The second part focused on the plea bargaining practice,
offering valuable insights for improving efficiency and managing cases effectively. The seminar was attended by all judges of the
Assize Courts and judges from Malta.



Seminar on the New Civil Procedure Rules (October 15)
Having marked one year since the entry into force of the new Civil Procedure Rules, the Cyprus Judicial Training School, in
collaboration with the Academy of the Law Office of the Republic and the Cyprus Bar Association, organized a seminar, in the form
of a panel discussion which aimed at identifying and addressing the challenges and difficulties faced by judges, prosecutors and
lawyers during the first year of application of the new Rules. The panelists focused primarily on the procedural difficulties that
had arisen or been identified since the Rules came into effect on September 1, 2023 and discussed solutions for their proper and
efficient implementation. The seminar provided an excellent opportunity for exchanging views and presenting practical solutions
to the challenges stemming from the application of the new Rules. Participants gained valuable insights and practical advice for
effectively managing these challenges.



Seminar: “Reference for Preliminary Ruling to the Court of Justice of the European Union and Unfair Terms”
(October 30-31)
Held over a period of two days, October 30-31, this seminar focused on the procedure for references for preliminary rulings
to the Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU) and unfair terms in consumer contracts. It was organized in collaboration
with Mr. Konstantinos Lykourgos, Judge and President of the Third Chamber at the CJEU, and included practical exercises and
case studies on the submission of questions to the CJEU and the legal framework regarding unfair terms under Council Directive
93/13/EEC. The participant judges of courts of first instance and legal officers of the two Supreme Courts, had the opportunity to
analyze and deepen their understanding on the application of EU law in national procedures. The Judges of the Court of Appeal also
received training, which focused on the analysis of case studies based on judgments of the CJEU and concluded with a round table
discussion.

Additionally, for the second time since October 2017, the Cyprus Judicial Training School organized the three-day the training program
“Judicial Training for Trainers – Judgecraft Training” aiming at training and preparing the School’s new generation of trainers. For
the design and delivery of this program, the School collaborated once again with Professor Jeremy Cooper, former Judge and former
Deputy Director of the Judicial College of England, and Ms. Michelle Austin, Principal Occupational Psychologist at the Judicial Office of
England and Wales.

Two seminars were delivered as part of the initial training for new judges, whilst eleven seminars were implemented under the
continuous training program for judges and court staff. With regards to 2024, a total of 287 participations in training activities were
recorded for Judges, 22 for Legal Officers, and 20 for Court Registrars

A significant portion of the costs for the implantation of training activities was covered by the Recovery and Resilience Plan, in which
the Judicial Training School participates for the period 2022–2025. The School’s commitment to the Plan includes training of at least
110 Judges on the new Civil Procedure Rules, as well as on other judicial skills.

The Cyprus Judicial Training School consistently supports the work of Cypriot judges, equipping them with the knowledge and skills
needed to meet the challenges of modern justice. Through its seminars and educational initiatives, the School fosters an environment
of continuous learning and reinforces the values of the rule of law. This ensures that judges remain impartial and well-prepared to
safeguard citizens’ rights and the transparency of the judicial system. In doing so, the School strengthens public trust in the institutions
and the administration of justice.

“The Court of
Appeal is largely

                     Yiasemis N. Yiasemis

                     Justice
                     Supreme Court



                     The Court of Appeal is the latest court established within the Judicial system of the
                     Republic of Cyprus. It was established by the Administration of Justice (Miscellaneous
                     Provisions) Law of 1964, L.33/1964, as amended, and in particular by the amending
                     Law 145(I)/2022. It assumed its duties on 1st July, 2023. The new Court of Appeal is a
                     Court of second instance, having jurisdiction to hear appeals against judgements of first
                     instance courts. For recourse to this Court there is no requirement for a permission
                     to appeal. Nonetheless, any questions of law which arise from its decisions can be
                     adjudicated upon, at third instance, after acquiring permission to do so on specific
                     grounds provided for in section 9 of said Law.

                     The sixteen Judges of the Court of Appeal, including its President, exercise the aforesaid
                     jurisdiction in all fields of law: Civil, Criminal and Administrative. Usually, they sit in
                     chambers of three. Nevertheless, under the express provision of section 11(5) of the said
                     Law, a single judge may adjudicate on an interim application made in an appeal or on
                     an appeal against an interim application, excluding an appeal concerning interlocutory
                     orders.

                     The routine handling of first instance disputes by the Court of Appeal, requires it to
                     directly deal with disputes of unsatisfied parties from courts of first instance. Hence,
                     the Judges of the Court of Appeal, must be knowledgeable and remain up to date with
                     applicable, substantive and procedural law. In this respect, the Court of Appeal, holds a
                     special status in the legal justice system of the Republic of Cyprus. Accordingly, it stands
                     out as a prominent court, as it makes final determination of facts, while its decisions bind
                     lower courts, on matters of law. Thus, the Court of Appeal is largely considered a court
                     of last instance.

                     The Court of Appeal operates on its own premises, though these are not a purpose-
                     built courthouse as they ought to be. Irrespectively, the Court is run by a competent
                     workforce who contribute to the appropriate daily functioning of the Court of Appeal.
                     The State must address the needs of the Court regarding premises and operations, to
                     ensure its continued efficiency. To do so, would undoubtedly enable its Judges to focus
                     on their judicial tasks, without extraneous interferences.
